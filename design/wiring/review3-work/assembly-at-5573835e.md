## 0. Words used here

- BLOCK: the flat top part of the ring that holds the screen. BAND: the hoop round the finger. SHOULDER: where the band flows into the block (`design/architecture.md:415`).
- BODY (HSG1): the one metal part that is both block and band (`design/architecture.md:420`).
- CHANNEL: the open groove inside the band, 7.0 mm wide (6.8 before inside model v8), open toward the finger (`design/architecture.md:440`).
- LINER (LNR1): the 0.3 mm resin skin cast into the channel. It closes the channel and touches the finger (`design/architecture.md:440`).
- RIBBON: a thin flexible circuit (FLEX1, FLEX2) that carries wires from the main board into the band (`design/architecture.md:441`).
- HARNESS: in this file, a ribbon with its band parts already soldered on (a word of this file, not of the design).
- REFLOW: soldering in an oven, where printed solder paste melts under every part at once.
- SWD: the two-wire programming port of the main chip (SWDCLK, SWDIO), reached through test pads (`design/architecture.md:291`).
- SHIP MODE: a state of the power chip in which the battery is cut off from everything and the ring draws almost nothing until it is woken (`design/datasheets/npm1304_datasheet_v1_0.txt:9059`).
- JIG: a holding fixture with spring pins that touch pads and connect a test computer.
- [PROPOSED]: this file proposes the process because no design file names one. The reason follows the tag. A proposal is not a design decision.
- [CALC]: a figure worked here from sourced numbers; the working is in the text.
- Face names on the main board. The board has parts on BOTH faces. The PARTS FACE is the KiCad face F.Cu, called "top" in `design/ecad/ring/pcb/fab/ring-pos-front.csv:1`; it carries 72 parts (the chips, the antenna ANT1 and the passives) and the two ribbon land rows J_BAND_A and J_BAND_B, and it faces the block floor. The older text of this file called it the "under face"; in this file the two names mean the same face. The SCREEN FACE is the KiCad face B.Cu, called "bottom" in `design/ecad/ring/pcb/fab/ring-pos-back.csv:1`; it carries the screen connector J1, 8 small parts (C1, C2, C5, C7, C14, FB1, R71, R72) and the 12 test pads, and it faces the screen (`design/ecad/ring/pcb/board-notes-r3.md:35`, `design/ecad/ring/pcb/placement-final.csv:78`). The KiCad words "top" and "bottom" do not say which face you see in the ring: use PARTS FACE and SCREEN FACE.

## 2. Assembly order

Every step has five fields: Join (what is joined to what, with which material or process), Locate (the face or feature that sets the position), Before (what must be true first), Check (what proves the step), After (what cannot be undone once the step is done). Steps 1 to 5 are the board, 6 to 9 the band harness, 10 to 13 the body, 14 to 18 the joining and the open test, 19 to 23 the closing, 24 to 28 the closed ring.

Steps that change because of the resin radio corner (`design/architecture.md:674`) carry a line "Radio corner:". The resin corner RC1 is in the design (the lead's default of 2026-11-13, which the owner can overrule, `design/architecture.md:676`): the body is the metal part with that corner cut away, and RC1 is its own part.

### Step 1. Accept the bare main board

- **Join:** nothing is joined. The bare board PCB1 (`main_board`) is accepted from the board maker.
- **Locate:** the outline 27.2 x 12.6 mm and no notch (`design/mfg/specs/main-board.md:27`, `design/mfg/specs/main-board.md:31`).
- **Before:** the board files exist. The ring board is routed: the rules check prints `Found 0 violations` and `Found 0 unconnected items` (`design/ecad/ring/pcb/board-notes-r3.md:20`), and U10, C81 and C82 are placed on it (`design/ecad/ring/pcb/fab/ring-pos-front.csv:34`, `design/ecad/ring/pcb/fab/ring-pos-front.csv:35`, `design/ecad/ring/pcb/fab/ring-pos-front.csv:72`).
- **Check:** the board maker's electrical test of every net, thickness, warp (`design/mfg/specs/main-board.md:45`); no hole through the board (`design/mfg/specs/main-board.md:34`; the light guide hole is removed in v7).
- **After:** nothing irreversible.

### Step 2. Print paste, place the board parts on both faces, reflow twice (screen face first, then parts face)

The board has parts on both faces (`design/ecad/ring/pcb/board-notes-r3.md:35`). The 12 test pads TP1, TP3, TP4, TP5, TP6, TP12, TP14, TP15, TP17, TP18, TP19 and TP21 are copper on this face and get no paste [PROPOSED: reason, a solder bump on a test pad spoils the spring-pin contact of step 5; the file `ring-B_Paste.gbr` as delivered has a round 0.80 mm opening at each of the 12 test pads (`design/wiring/guide-fix-work/paste_count.log`), so the stencil maker closes those 12 openings]. The motor switch and the motor diode of earlier versions are not fitted: they left the design (`design/architecture.md:705`). LED1, R41 and Q2 are removed in v7 and are not fitted (`design/architecture.md:834`). C13 is not mounted (`design/bom.csv:18`). ORDER OF THE TWO PASSES [PROPOSED: the lead confirms]: the screen face goes first and the parts face second. Reason: the face reflowed first hangs face down during the second reflow, and the melted solder holds a part there only if the part is small; the screen face has 9 parts, all small (eight 0201 parts and the connector), and the parts face has 72, among them U1 and the 1.00 mm parts (`design/architecture.md:426`). - **Locate:** fiducials of the board file; each part's land pattern (from the routed board file, `design/ecad/ring/pcb/board-notes-r3.md:20`); in pass 2 the board lies on its outline in the carrier.
- **Before:** step 1 passed. The antenna mount is decided: ANT1 is soldered on its maker's land pattern on the parts face, with no notch (before model v9, ANT1 stood in a corner notch with its pads off the board, `design/architecture.md:513`, open item S15). Before pass 2: the pass 1 check below has passed.
- **Check:** after PASS 1, optical inspection of the screen face at 10 times: J1 sits flat on the board with none of its 15 contact tails and its 2 mounting tails lifted, no solder bridge between neighbouring contacts (0.3 mm pitch), each of the eight 0201 parts in place and not tilted, no solder on any of the 12 test pads. After PASS 2, X-ray of U1 (61 balls), U2, U4 and the U3 lands; optical inspection of every other part of the parts face; then the screen face again: all 9 parts still in place, J1 still flat. A screen face part that fell in pass 2 is put back by hand soldering on that face (the ring is not reflowed a third time) [PROPOSED]. The number of reflow passes that J1 and the 0201 parts accept is not in any file cited here: the parts see two reflows (open, for the lead).
- **After:** reflow is not undone. A parts face part can be reworked by hand until step 17; a screen face part until step 15, because from step 15 the screen's cable lies over that face.

- **Locate:** the board's parts face as zero; the lid of U5 and of U6.
- **Before:** step 2 passed.
- **Check:** the gap g = lid Z - 0.300; gasket 0.10 (Nitto SCF AN010) if g is under 0.07, 0.20 (SCF400T) if g is 0.07 to 0.14, 0.30 (SCF400) if g is over 0.14 (`design/mech/mic-path/mic-path.md:152`, all three start values, not settled by a document: Nitto's sheets with the compression values could not be downloaded; bench B7 measures the leak). ## 4. Water and dust (F11)

What IP65 asks, in plain words. The first digit 6 means dust-tight: "No ingress of dust; complete protection against contact (dust-tight). A vacuum must be applied. Test duration of up to 8 hours based on airflow." The second digit 5 means water jets: "Water projected by a nozzle ( 6.3 mm (0.25 in) ) against enclosure from any direction shall have no harmful effects", for "1 minute per square meter for at least 3 minutes", with "12.5 litres per minute", at a pressure of 30 kPa at a distance of 3 m. Source: the Wikipedia page "IP code" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IP_code), a summary of the standard IEC 60529, read on 2026-11-11; the standard itself was not read. The requirement S6 was "IP65 or better" when signed; since the owner's decision of 2026-11-13 it reads "Splash and sweat resistant as a goal", with no rating claimed (`requirements.md:26`). The test that would prove IP65 (to describe, not to order: bench purchases are on hold). On finished rings, after step 24 (sample size from the test house [PROPOSED: at least 3 rings, one per size group: US 7, US 10, US 13]):
1. Before: all factory tests of step 25 pass, and the screen shows no mist.
2. Dust (first digit 6): the dust chamber of IEC 60529 for the 6, with a vacuum applied, up to 8 hours.
3. Water jets (second digit 5): the 6.3 mm nozzle, 12.5 litres per minute, 30 kPa at 3 m, from every direction, 1 minute per square metre and at least 3 minutes; the ring turned so the jet reaches the joystick, the microphone holes, the liner edges and the pads.
4. After: all factory tests pass again; no mist under the glass; then one ring is cut open on the seams of rows 1, 2, 5 and 6 to look for dust and water traces [PROPOSED: reason, the ring has no port, so only a cut shows what got in].
A pass on all rings in both tests gives IP65. Water by immersion (IPX7 or IPX8) is a separate test; IPX7 does not imply IPX5 (same source).
