Hand derivations of review 4 (my reasoning, from the numbers in pads/ and the cited files).

1. Tail files are a turn of the ring view, not a mirror.
   Ring frame seen from +Z (above): X right, Y up (right-handed, Z toward the viewer). KiCad file view: x right, y down.
   FLEX1 (pads/flex1-us9-pads.csv): u = x - 50.0, v = 80.0 - y; ring X = v - 2.5, Y = -(3.4 + u) (band-interface.md:65).
   KiCad right (+x) -> ring -Y (down the page seen from above); KiCad down (+y) -> v falls -> ring -X (left).
   right->down and down->left is a 90 degree clockwise turn: no mirror.
   FLEX2 (pads/flex2-us9-pads.csv): v = y - 80.0, Y = +(3.4 + u). KiCad right -> ring +Y (up); KiCad down -> ring +X (right).
   right->up and down->right is a 90 degree counter-clockwise turn: no mirror.
   The tail lands are on F.Cu, which faces +Z (the board) in the ring, so the KiCad view of the tail is the view from +Z: consistent.
   check_all.py uses exactly this map: 105 of 105 lands within 0.05 mm (check_all.log, INFO mating 105).

2. Bench posture of step 14 against the ring.
   Bench: board parts face (F.Cu) up, seen as the board file (x right, y down), J_BAND_A (ring Y -4.325) in the upper row.
   Turning the joined board 180 degrees about X (step 15) maps a point (x, y, z) to (x, -y, -z): the upper row goes to the
   lower side of the page; seen from above the ring frame has +Y up, so J_BAND_A ends at ring -Y, the FLEX1 slot side
   (Y -5.9). The feet turn with the board (rigid turn), so no ribbon is twisted and the lands still face the board.

   Which J1 land row faces the cable entry: first not settled at 80 dpi; settled in item 5 from the 220 dpi crop.
