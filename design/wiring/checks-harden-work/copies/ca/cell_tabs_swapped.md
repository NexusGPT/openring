## 0. Words used here

- BLOCK: the flat top part of the ring that holds the screen. BAND: the hoop round the finger. SHOULDER: where the band flows into the block (`design/architecture.md:415`).
- BODY (HSG1): the one metal part that is both block and band (`design/architecture.md:420`).
- CHANNEL: the open groove inside the band, 7.0 mm wide (6.8 before inside model v8), open toward the finger (`design/architecture.md:440`).
- LINER (LNR1): the 0.3 mm resin skin cast into the channel. It closes the channel and touches the finger (`design/architecture.md:440`).
- RIBBON: a thin flexible circuit (FLEX1, FLEX2) that carries wires from the main board into the band (`design/architecture.md:441`).
- HARNESS: in this file, a ribbon with its band parts already soldered on (a word of this file, not of the design).
- REFLOW: soldering in an oven, where printed solder paste melts under every part at once.
- SWD: the two-wire programming port of the main chip (SWDCLK, SWDIO), reached through test pads (`design/architecture.md:291`).
- SHIP MODE: a state of the power chip in which the battery is cut off from everything and the ring draws almost nothing until it is woken (`design/datasheets/npm1304_datasheet_v1_0.txt:9059`).
- JIG: a holding fixture with spring pins that touch pads and connect a test computer.
- [PROPOSED]: this file proposes the process because no design file names one. The reason follows the tag. A proposal is not a design decision.
- [CALC]: a figure worked here from sourced numbers; the working is in the text.
- Face names on the main board. The board has parts on BOTH faces. The PARTS FACE is the KiCad face F.Cu, called "top" in `design/ecad/ring/pcb/fab/ring-pos-front.csv:1`; it carries 72 parts (the chips, the antenna ANT1 and the passives) and the two ribbon land rows J_BAND_A and J_BAND_B, and it faces the block floor. The older text of this file called it the "under face"; in this file the two names mean the same face. The SCREEN FACE is the KiCad face B.Cu, called "bottom" in `design/ecad/ring/pcb/fab/ring-pos-back.csv:1`; it carries the screen connector J1, 8 small parts (C1, C2, C5, C7, C14, FB1, R71, R72) and the 12 test pads, and it faces the screen (`design/ecad/ring/pcb/board-notes-r3.md:35`; J1: `design/ecad/ring/pcb/placement-final.csv:80`). The KiCad words "top" and "bottom" do not say which face you see in the ring: use PARTS FACE and SCREEN FACE.

## 2. Assembly order

Every step has five fields: Join (what is joined to what, with which material or process), Locate (the face or feature that sets the position), Before (what must be true first), Check (what proves the step), After (what cannot be undone once the step is done). Steps 1 to 5 are the board, 6 to 9 the band harness, 10 to 13 the body, 14 to 18 the joining and the open test, 19 to 23 the closing, 24 to 28 the closed ring.

Steps that change because of the resin radio corner (`design/architecture.md:674`) carry a line "Radio corner:". The resin corner RC1 is in the design (the lead's default of 2026-11-13, which the owner can overrule, `design/architecture.md:676`): the body is the metal part with that corner cut away, and RC1 is its own part.

### Step 1. Accept the bare main board

- **Join:** nothing is joined. The bare board PCB1 (`main_board`) is accepted from the board maker.
- **Locate:** the outline 27.2 x 12.6 mm and no notch (`design/mfg/specs/main-board.md:27`, `design/mfg/specs/main-board.md:31`).
- **Before:** the board files exist. The ring board is routed: the rules check prints `Found 0 violations` and `Found 0 unconnected items` (`design/ecad/ring/pcb/board-notes-r3.md:20`), and U10, C81 and C82 are placed on it (`design/ecad/ring/pcb/fab/ring-pos-front.csv:39`, `design/ecad/ring/pcb/fab/ring-pos-front.csv:40`, `design/ecad/ring/pcb/fab/ring-pos-front.csv:83`).
- **Check:** the board maker's electrical test of every net, thickness, warp (`design/mfg/specs/main-board.md:45`); no hole through the board (`design/mfg/specs/main-board.md:34`; the light guide hole is removed in v7).
- **After:** nothing irreversible.

### Step 2. Print paste, place the board parts on both faces, reflow twice (screen face first, then parts face)

The board has parts on both faces (`design/ecad/ring/pcb/board-notes-r3.md:35`). PASS 1 is the SCREEN FACE and PASS 2 the PARTS FACE (`design/mfg/assembly/process.md:84`, `design/mfg/assembly/process.md:85`; confirmed by the lead, `design/wiring/lead-decisions.md:82`). Squeegee: a steel or polyurethane blade of 80 to 90 durometer (`design/mfg/assembly/process.md:171`).
- **Reflow profile (both passes):** a thermocouple is taped on the board surface beside the most massive part, and the oven follows this profile on it (`design/mfg/assembly/process.md:104`). 1. Ramp from room temperature to 150 C at less than 2.5 C per second. 2. Soak from 150 C to 200 C in 90 to 110 s, then go on to 217 C. 3. Stay above 217 C for 60 to 75 s. 4. Stay above 230 C for at most 35 s. 5. Peak 240 C; the thermocouple must read 235 to 245 C. 6. Cool at 6 C per second or slower. Do not move the board while the solder is liquid (`design/mfg/assembly/process.md:105` to `design/mfg/assembly/process.md:110`).
- **Never a third reflow:** J1 accepts at most 2 reflow cycles, and the two passes use both. Hirose's drawing says "Number of reflow cycles:2 cycles MAX." (`design/datasheets/w1_hirose_fh35c-15s-0.3shw50_drawing.pdf` page 6, an image page; `design/mfg/assembly/process.md:116`). So the ring board never goes through an oven a third time. The order of the passes: the face reflowed first hangs face down during the second reflow, and the melted solder holds a part there only if the part is small. The screen face has 9 parts, all small (eight 0201 parts and the connector); the parts face has 72, among them U1 and the 1.00 mm parts (`design/architecture.md:426`). How the screen face parts stay on during pass 2: the board lies on a carrier with the screen face down, flat, with a pocket under J1 (`design/mfg/assembly/process.md:170`; the housing is 0.9 mm high, `design/wiring/screen-cable-study.md:59`). Nobody touches or moves the board while the solder is liquid. The board is not turned over until it is cold.
- **Locate:** the board itself carries no fiducial (`design/mfg/assembly/process.md:185`). By hand: lay the stencil on the board and move it until every opening sits over its land, seen under the 10 times magnifier (`design/mfg/assembly/process.md:189`). By a placing machine: the three 1 mm fiducials on the panel rails of the face being printed, 3.85 mm from the panel edges (`design/mfg/assembly/process.md:186`; change C4, the panel is not drawn yet). Each part sits on its land pattern of the routed board file (`design/ecad/ring/pcb/board-notes-r3.md:20`). In pass 2 the board lies on its outline in the carrier with the pocket under J1.
- **Before:** step 1 passed. The antenna mount is decided: ANT1 is soldered on its maker's land pattern on the parts face, with no notch (before model v9, ANT1 stood in a corner notch with its pads off the board, `design/architecture.md:513`, open item S15). U3 stays in its sealed bag until it is placed (moisture class 3, `design/mfg/assembly/process.md:120`). Before pass 2: the pass 1 check below has passed.
- **Check:** every joint of each pass is judged by these accept rules, under the 10 times magnifier (the lead's wording, after the common workmanship practice for class 2 electronics, `design/wiring/lead-decisions.md:82`). A joint passes only when all of them hold. 1. The paste has melted and flowed: the solder is smooth and joined up, with no grainy paste left. 2. The solder wets both the land and the metal end or lead of the part: its edge runs out thin onto both, in a hollow curve. 3. No ball of solder sits on top of a land without wetting it, and no bare metal ring shows round the solder. 4. No solder joins two lands that must be apart. 5. No loose solder ball lies next to the joint. 6. The part lies flat on its lands, square to them, and does not stand up on one end. 7. Each end of the part lies on its own land; no end hangs off its land. 8. No crack and no hole shows in the solder. For example, each end of ANT1 has a wetted end with a hollow curve of solder onto its land. Parts whose joints lie under their body cannot be seen: they are X-rayed (below). After PASS 1, also: J1 sits flat on the board with none of its 15 contact tails and its 2 mounting tails lifted; no solder bridge between neighbouring contacts (0.3 mm pitch); each of the eight 0201 parts in place and not tilted; no solder on any of the 12 test pads. After PASS 2, also: X-ray of U1 (61 balls), U2, U4 and the U3 lands, each ball round, of the same size as its neighbours, joined to its land and to no other; each of the 15 ribbon lands carries a smooth solder dome, and no solder joins two of them (`design/mfg/assembly/hotbar-joint.md:171`); then the screen face again: all 9 parts still in place, J1 still flat. Of the screen face parts only J1 may be put right by hand after pass 2; the board is never reflowed a third time (`design/mfg/assembly/process.md:116`). J1 itself may be reworked with an iron at 350 C for at most 5 s (Hirose, `design/mfg/assembly/process.md:116`). The process sheet gives no iron setting for the eight 0201 parts (its hand soldering table, `design/mfg/assembly/process.md:148` to `design/mfg/assembly/process.md:155`, has no row for them), and the board may not go back through the oven to put a part right (`design/mfg/assembly/process.md:116`: "No third reflow of the ring board, ever"): so a board on which one of these eight parts fell, tilted or moved in pass 2 is NOT repaired. Mark it "0201 fault", set it aside and take the next board (lead's decision, `design/wiring/lead-decisions.md`, row "fallen 0201 part on the screen face").
- **After:** reflow is not undone, and the board never sees an oven again. A parts face part can be reworked by hand until step 15, when the board goes into the block. A screen face part can be reworked until step 16, because from step 16 the screen's cable lies over that face.

## 4. Water and dust (F11)

What IP65 asks, in plain words. The first digit 6 means dust-tight: "No ingress of dust; complete protection against contact (dust-tight). A vacuum must be applied. Test duration of up to 8 hours based on airflow." The second digit 5 means water jets: "Water projected by a nozzle ( 6.3 mm (0.25 in) ) against enclosure from any direction shall have no harmful effects", for "1 minute per square meter for at least 3 minutes", with "12.5 litres per minute", at a pressure of 30 kPa at a distance of 3 m. Source: the Wikipedia page "IP code" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IP_code), a summary of the standard IEC 60529, read on 2026-11-11; the standard itself was not read. The requirement S6 was "IP65 or better" when signed; since the owner's decision of 2026-11-13 it reads "Splash and sweat resistant as a goal", with no rating claimed (`requirements.md:26`). The test that would prove IP65 (to describe, not to order: bench purchases are on hold). On finished rings, after step 24 (sample size from the test house [PROPOSED: at least 3 rings, one per size group: US 7, US 10, US 13]):
1. Before: all factory tests of step 25 pass, and the screen shows no mist.
2. Dust (first digit 6): the dust chamber of IEC 60529 for the 6, with a vacuum applied, up to 8 hours.
3. Water jets (second digit 5): the 6.3 mm nozzle, 12.5 litres per minute, 30 kPa at 3 m, from every direction, 1 minute per square metre and at least 3 minutes; the ring turned so the jet reaches the joystick, the microphone holes, the liner edges and the pads.
4. After: all factory tests pass again; no mist under the glass; then one ring is cut open on the seams of rows 1, 2, 5 and 6 to look for dust and water traces [PROPOSED: reason, the ring has no port, so only a cut shows what got in].
A pass on all rings in both tests gives IP65. Water by immersion (IPX7 or IPX8) is a separate test; IPX7 does not imply IPX5 (same source).
