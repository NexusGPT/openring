### 11.2 What protects the charging pads on the skin (change c)

The pads CP1 to CP4 touch the finger (17.12). Five statements, each checked:
1. VBUS is an input only. The only source on the net VBUS is the pad CP1 (the case); U2's VBUS ball (power in), U1 ball H9 (through R1) and U9 are loads (check A6-3). The ring's own supply output VBUSOUT is a separate net that touches no pad. With no case, no voltage is on the pad.
2. D- and D+: the nets USB_DN and USB_DP hold only U1 balls H6 and H7 (the chip's dedicated USB pads), U9, the pads CP2 and CP3 and the tail pad: no pull-up, no pull-down, no series part (check A6-3). So when no case is present they are held by the chip alone, as 17.12 says. WHAT THE CHIP DOES WITH THESE TWO PADS while VBUS is absent and USB is off is NOT stated in any source in the project: architecture 17.12 says "held by the chip" with no source, and Nordic's product specification chapter for the USB pins is not saved here (it is HTML only, `parts/mcu.md` line 8). Open point O4. A line with no source and a high impedance puts no current into skin; the exposure is static discharge and sweat leakage, which is U9's job.
3. U9 TPD4E05U06 is still the right part for that job: IEC 61000-4-2 level 4, 12 kV contact and 15 kV air discharge (`parts/codes_tpd4e05u06_text.log` line @@tpd_esd@@ and the ESD table), reverse stand-off 5.5 V (line @@tpd_vr@@, above the 5 V of USB), leakage 10 nA at 2.5 V (line @@tpd_leak@@), input capacitance 0.42 to 0.5 pF (line @@tpd_cap@@, low enough for the USB data lines). A finger discharge is within that rating.
4. Its limits: it clamps at the board end of J_BAND_B; the flex between the pads and the board has no clamp, so U9 must sit next to the J_BAND_B pads (the board's business, `pcb/board-notes.md`). Both D2 channels are on VBUS, so a strike on the VBUS pad is clamped to ground by two channels. A VBUS above 5.5 V on the pad is outside U9's stand-off: the case must deliver 5 V only (case designer).
5. Not changed: no part is added at the pads, because the pads are in the resin liner and no source says a part fits there (open point O6).

### 11.3 Review F07 (change d): how it was settled and the source of each fact

- The main chip: one supply for every I/O. The CAAA package has two VDD balls (A4, E10), no other supply ball for I/O (`parts/nrf54lm20a-csp61-pins.csv`: the supply-type names are VDD, VSS, VBUS and the decoupling balls DECD, DECA, DECRF, DECUSB). Nordic's reference layout says "VDD:nRF supplied by 1.8 V to 3.6 V" (`parts/nordic-ref-text/caaa_schematic.txt` line @@sch55@@). So the chip's I/O for D9, A9, B10, D10 follows A4 and E10, which are on VDD_1V8 (1.8 V). The chip's I/O CAN run at 3.0 V only if A4 and E10 are moved to VDD_3V0, which moves every chip pin to 3.0 V: the microphone clock into 1.8 V microphones (VDD_MIC), the power chip's I2C (VDDIO), and the screen's IOVCC. That is a design change to the whole rail plan, not a wiring detail: not done here.
- The chip's input limit: any I/O pin from -0.3 V to VDD + 0.3 V (the nRF54L15 document of another maker, IndieSemiC ISC-nRF54L15-A, `datasheets/mcu_nrf54l15_ds_v2.2.pdf` page 15, saved text line @@nrf_abs@@ of `evidence/a6/mcu_nrf54l15_ds_v2.2.txt`). Nordic's own nRF54LM20A product specification is not in the project; this limit is that document's, for a sibling chip, and is an assumption for the nRF54LM20A.
- The storage part: supply 2.7 to 3.6 V (maker spec `datasheets/slim2_cs-sdnand-20230403.pdf` page 4, saved text line @@cs_vcc@@) and input high VIH min 0.625 x VCC, max VCC + 0.3 V (page 8, line @@cs_vih@@): 1.875 V at 3.0 V. A 1.8 V chip high is below that, so the chip's outputs do not meet the storage part's input high. In the other direction the storage part's output high (VOH min 0.75 x VCC = 2.25 V, same page) and the 47 kohm pull-ups to 3.0 V exceed VDD + 0.3 V = 2.1 V on D10 and D9. F07 is confirmed, both ways.
- Option "storage at 1.8 V": the CSNP32GCR01-BOW has one supply pad (VCC) and its maker spec gives 2.7 to 3.6 V only; the file `datasheets/slim2_cs-csnp32gcr01-20260408.pdf` holds the XTX XTSDx0xG datasheet (1.8 V and 3.3 V series, another maker's family), not the CSNP32GCR01-BOW. Not an option.
- So a level shifter: TI SN74AXC4T774, orderable code SN74AXC4T774RSVR (UQFN-16 RSV, 2.6 x 1.8 mm, 3000 per reel, Active, Production), read from TI's datasheet SCES898C fetched from `https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/sn74axc4t774.pdf` (HTTP 200, `evidence/a6/curl_axc4t774.log`; copy `evidence/a6/dl_ti/ti_sn74axc4t774.pdf`, text `evidence/a6/ti_sn74axc4t774.txt`, orderable table line @@ti_ord@@, pin table line @@ti_pin@@). Each port 0.65 to 3.6 V; independent direction pins (function table line @@ti_fn@@: DIR high = A to B); VCC isolation: below 100 mV on either supply every port is Hi-Z (line @@ti_iso@@), so the part is safe while VDD_SD is switched off; 0.1 uF bypass on each supply (line @@ti_bp@@). Wiring: VCCA on VDD_1V8, VCCB on VDD_SD, DIR1 to DIR3 on VDD_1V8 (A to B: SCK, MOSI, CS), DIR4 on ground (B to A: MISO), OE on ground; A side = the chip nets, B side = the card nets with the W3 pull-ups. No supplier was contacted; the part was read, not priced.
- Open point O3: the lead confirms this choice (the alternative, VDD of the main chip on 3.0 V, is a rail-plan change and is not taken). The board needs U10, C81, C82 (the board file is read-only here).

### 11.4 Review F14 (change g): the main chip's ball grid

Statement 1: `design/ecad/ring/pcb/board-notes.md` line @@bn_u1@@: the ball names "A to H x 1 to 10 are an 8 x 10 grid of 80 sites". Statement 2: `design/mech/thin/placement.csv` line @@pl_u1@@: "61 balls: 9 x 7 grid less 2 corners ... dia 0.2, pitch 0.325". Maker source in the project: Nordic's CAAA reference schematic (`design/datasheets/nrf54lm20x_caaa_ref_schematic_0_2.pdf`, copied into `design/parts/nrf54lm20a-csp61-pins.csv`), which names every ball; the reference layout `nrf54lm20x_caaa_ref_pcb_0_2.pdf` page 1 is a picture without dimensions (rendered, `evidence/a6/caaa_pcb_png/p1_u1_zoom2.png`), and the package drawing is not saved (`parts/mcu.md` line @@mcu68@@).
Verdict. The grid of ball NAMES is right: the 61 names use rows A to H and columns 1 to 10 (counted from the csv: A 9, B 8, C 1, D 7, E 9, F 9, G 9, H 9 balls; 19 of the 80 sites empty), so they cannot sit on a 9 x 7 lattice (63 sites, no row H, no column 10). Statement 2 is wrong for the names. The PITCH is not settled and statement 2's pitch is wrong for an 8 x 10 lattice: 9 x 0.325 + 0.2 = 3.125 mm is wider than the 3.02 mm body, and 7 x 0.325 + 0.2 = 2.475 mm is taller than 2.43 mm. For 10 columns in 3.017 mm the pitch is at most (3.017 - 0.2) / 9 = 0.313 mm. (My reading, not confirmed: `parts/mcu.md` line @@mcu68@@ gives a nominal BALL DIAMETER of about 0.325 mm, and that may be where the 0.325 came from.) Open point O5: Nordic's CSP61 (CAAA) package drawing (ball pitch, ball diameter, A1 corner and orientation); then the board owner corrects the footprint. This file does not change the pcb folder.
