## 1. Central choice: USB host plus Wi-Fi in one part

| Candidate | USB host | Wi-Fi | Fact that decides | Verdict |

| Espressif ESP32-P4 + ESP32-C6 module | "USB 2.0 High-Speed OTG" [DS case_esp32p4_ds.pdf p.4] | **none in the chip**: "Wi-Fi" appears only in the trademark notice of the 10,450-line text [DS, evidence/read_p4.log] | Needs a second chip for Wi-Fi, QFN104 10 x 10 mm [DS p4 p.1], external flash; saves 2.50 min per recorded hour only | rejected (see notes) |

**Transfer time, stated plainly [CALC, evidence/calc_case.log]:**
- One recorded hour: 16,000 x 2 bytes x 2 channels x 3,600 s = 230.4 MB of audio (the ring has 2 microphones, `design/architecture.md` 16.6). The 464.4 MB behind the C2 figure 6.37 min was the old 4-microphone figure (460.8 MB plus 0.78 % block headers: 464.4 / 460.8 = 1.0078). Stored bytes (r4, following `design/firmware.md` line 718): 3,600 s / 0.512 s = 7,031.25 chunks x 33,280 bytes = **234.0 MB per recorded hour** [CALC] (C3 had 232.2 MB from the old header ratio).
- At the full-speed line rate of 12 Mbit/s (1.500 MB/s): 234.0 / 1.500 = 156.0 s = **2.60 min**. This is a bound the bus never reaches.
- Full-speed bulk ceiling: 19 packets x 64 bytes per 1 ms frame = 1.216 MB/s (USB 2.0 figure, LIST). 234.0 / 1.216 = 192.4 s = **3.21 min per recorded hour. This is the planning figure for the chosen part** (`design/firmware.md` line 718). A 10-hour day: 32.1 min. A full 4 GB card (16.9 hours, `design/firmware.md` line 718): 54.2 min. The ring's cell limits recording to 4.3 to 7.8 hours by ring size (`requirements.md` row P2): 4.3 x 3.21 = 13.8 to 7.8 x 3.21 = 25.0 min.
- High speed through ESP32-P4: the ring's storage read over SPI (2 MB/s, architecture.md section 8) is the limit: 234.0 / 2.0 = 117.0 s = 1.95 min per recorded hour. The gain is 1.26 min per recorded hour.
- A ring charge at 1 C (the charge current is open item S12) takes about 60 min of constant current. In the 60 minutes a ring charge takes, the case copies 18.7 recorded hours (60 / 3.21), more than the ring can hold; the 4.3 to 7.8 recorded hours of one battery are copied in 13.8 to 25.0 min.
- Condition: the ring's nRF54LM20A must run its high-speed USB device at full speed when the host does not answer the high-speed handshake. USB 2.0 requires this of every high-speed device (LIST). Nordic's USBHS page was not readable (`design/parts/mcu.md` line 71). Bench item B1.

## 5. Sequence, step by step

1. Ring set into the pocket and around the post, lid closed: the two magnets hold the lid; the band's bottom rests on the floor and its outside on the two flanks of the V wall; each pin stands at 1.98 to 2.15 mm (28 values over 4 pins and 7 sizes, design/wiring/ring-case-contact.csv) (Harwin P70-6000045R, working height 2.10 mm).
2. U1 sleeps in Deep-sleep with the touch channel IO4 monitored. The ring's metal band over electrode E1 (on PCB2's front face, 2.04 to 2.20 mm from the band's inner face on the strip's centre line, design/wiring/g9-g11-work/e1_gap.log; REPLACED 2026-11-13 (was 2.28 to 2.42 mm): bench B3) raises the touch reading above the stored empty value: U1 wakes.
3. U1 reads the USB-C state from CC1/CC2 (ADC). No source: U1 sets the OTG pin high and the OTG_CONFIG bit to 1 [DS bq25895 section 8, "OTG pin HIGH and OTG_CONFIG bit =1"], BOOSTV code 7; with a source: no boost, PMID follows USB VBUS.
4. U1 sets U5 EN high: VRING rises at P1. U1 watches VRING sense (IO5) and FAULT (IO16). FAULT low or VRING below 4.5 V after 50 ms: EN low, light red blink (short or wrong object).
5. Ring's charger starts on VBUS. The ring's USB device pulls D+ up: U1, as USB host, sees the attach and enumerates (full speed). No attach within 3 s: EN low, light amber blink 3 times ("turn the ring over": a ring flipped over is not blocked by the pocket at every size), back to sleep.
6. U1 asks the ring for its file list and copies new recordings to U4 (SD 4-bit). Light: blue breathing. Speed 1.216 MB/s at most: 3.21 min per recorded hour.
7. After each file: U1 checks the file length and checksum against the ring's list, then tells the ring the file is safe; the ring may delete it.
8. Charging continues while VRING is on. The ring reports its charge state over USB. At full: EN low, OTG off. Light: green steady 10 s, then off.
9. Upload: when Wi-Fi is known and either USB-C is plugged or the ring charge is done, U1 uploads files from U4 over Wi-Fi, then deletes them from U4 after the server confirms. Light: blue slow blink.
10. Case charging: USB-C plugged: BQ25895 charges BT1 at 350 mA (0.5C), input limit from the CC reading. Light: amber steady while charging the case only.
11. Case low (BATV below 3.40 V): no boost, red blink every 5 s while the ring sits in the case.
12. Lid swung open and ring lifted: touch reading falls, USB detach: EN low, OTG off, U1 back to Deep-sleep.

The skin is three lofted B-spline solids (plate z 0 to 3.0, shell 3.0 to 37.66, lid 37.96 to 41.4 mm), each with planar end faces that are inner or seam faces; no boolean runs on the skin. Radii the inside allows, as asked: bottom edge 7.5 x 3.1 mm (a larger vertical radius would cut the cell's lower corners), top edge 10.0 x 1.8 mm (the lid thickness above the seam, 3.4 mm at the crown, and a 0.5 mm wall above the seam leave that), plan corners 5.6 mm.

**Finish and Wi-Fi (conflict K2, proposal to confirm with a maker).** A metal shell would screen the case's Wi-Fi antenna (U1's module antenna sits inside the shell), so the shell and lid are NOT metal. Proposal 1 (the one the pictures show): a moulded polymer body (PC/ABS or PC, 1.2 mm wall) with a physical-vapour-deposited satin metal layer a few tens of nanometres thick under a satin clear coat; at that thickness the layer is discontinuous at 2.4 GHz and does not screen (non-conductive vacuum metallisation, NCVM, is the maker's name for it). Cost in looks: a slightly softer reflection than solid metal and less cold weight; it can chip at the USB-C mouth. Proposal 2: a ceramic-like coating (anodised-look or PVD ceramic) on the polymer; same Wi-Fi result, harder surface, less metallic. Alternative if the owner wants real metal: a satin aluminium shell with a polymer window over the antenna end (the +X side, about 20 x 12 mm), which shows as a visible patch of another material in the pebble: that costs the one-piece look. Not tested here: no sample, no range test; both proposals are [LIST] and are supplier item S7 (section 8).
