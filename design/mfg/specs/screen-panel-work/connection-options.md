## The screen-face parts in the thin block (added after the lead's note)

The routed board carries on its screen face J1 and eight 0201 parts, C1, C2, C5, C7, C14, FB1, R71, R72, plus 12 flat test pads (`design/mfg/specs/screen-panel-work/logs/read_face_b.log:2` to `design/mfg/specs/screen-panel-work/logs/read_face_b.log:12`). The 0201 height used by the inside model is 0.33, marked ASSUMED there (`design/mech/thin/model-v10-notes.md:76`). The thin stack gives 0.10 of foam between the board top and the panel (`design/mech/thin/out/v10/before/placement.csv:23`), so as the board stands neither J1 nor the 0201 parts fit (`design/mech/thin/model-v10-notes.md:330`).

Point one, what it takes to leave them where they are (board top 1.75 to panel front 2.65 is 0.9: `design/mfg/specs/screen-panel-work/logs/calc.log:58`):
- The eight 0201 parts stay if the panel's back is at least land 0.02 + part 0.33 + clearance 0.05 over the board top, at 2.15: gap 0.4, panel at most 0.5 (`design/mfg/specs/screen-panel-work/logs/calc.log:59`; the 0.05 clearance is the one the inside model keeps over J1: `design/mech/thin/model-v10-notes.md:96`). With a clearance of 0.10 instead: panel at most 0.45 (`design/mfg/specs/screen-panel-work/logs/calc.log:60`). The foam frame round the board edge then is 0.4 thick instead of 0.10.
- J1 cannot stay on the screen face at any panel thickness: it needs 0.97 above the board at its nominal 0.9 and 1.07 at its maximum, against the 0.9 room: panel at most -0.07 or -0.17 (`design/mfg/specs/screen-panel-work/logs/calc.log:61`, `design/mfg/specs/screen-panel-work/logs/calc.log:62`). So J1 moves in every option.
- Is a panel of 0.5 or less plausible from the research on disk? Every module thickness read from a datasheet is 0.7 or more: 0.8 (`design/parts/thin-bezel-parts.md:37`), 0.75 (`design/parts/thin-bezel-parts.md:38`), 0.71 typical and 0.80 maximum for a flexible folded-drive part (`design/parts/thin-bezel-parts.md:39`), 0.78 (`design/parts/thin-bezel-parts.md:40`), 0.7 (`design/parts/thin-bezel-parts.md:41`), and a maker overview's "thinnest thickness of 0.7 mm" (`design/parts/full-face-screen.md:107`). The only figures under 0.7 are a search snippet of 0.5 that could not be read on its page (`design/parts/full-face-screen.md:87`, `design/parts/full-face-screen.md:128`) and a listing of 0.3 whose outline equals its lit area, marked suspicious (`design/parts/full-face-screen.md:96`). So 0.5 is not shown possible by the research: it must be asked of the maker.

Point two, if they cannot stay: J1 and the eight 0201 parts go back to the under face, with J1's 15 contacts (`design/ecad/ring/sch-notes.md:38`) on 11 nets plus ground (`design/mfg/specs/screen-panel-work/logs/calc.log:51`) and the 0201 parts' 6 nets DECA, DECD, GND, VDD_1V8, VSET1, VSET2 (`design/mfg/specs/screen-panel-work/logs/read_face_b.log:11`). ## Option B: tail soldered by hot bar to lands on the board's under face (no connector)

What: the path of A, but the tail's contact end is pressed by a hot bar onto lands on the under face at the hand end, as the two band ribbons are joined (`design/mfg/assembly/hotbar-joint.md:9`, 15 lands there).

- Where: the strip X -13.6 to -12.4, Y -2.65 to 2.35 has no courtyard (`design/mfg/specs/screen-panel-work/logs/read_board2.log:31`); the edge band rule keeps tracks and vias out up to X -13.45 (`design/mfg/specs/screen-panel-work/logs/read_board2.log:102`), so the lands sit from X -13.45 inward.
- Land row: at the hot-bar pitch this design uses, 0.30 lands at 0.51 pitch (`design/mfg/assembly/hotbar-joint.md:36`), 15 lands make a row 7.44 long (`design/mfg/specs/screen-panel-work/logs/calc.log:46`): wider than the 5.0 tail (`design/mfg/specs/screen-panel-work/logs/calc.log:45`) and the 5.4 pocket (`design/mech/thin/out/v10/before/thin.py:530`). At 0.3 pitch with 0.15 lands the row is 4.35 (`design/mfg/specs/screen-panel-work/logs/calc.log:47`) and fits a 4.8 tail (`design/wiring/screen-cable-study.md:55`), but no file of this design gives hot-bar data at that pitch: the maker or a joint test decides.
- Height: flex 0.11 + solder 0.03 = 0.14 under the board (`design/mfg/specs/screen-panel-work/logs/calc.log:55`), against J1's 0.9 (`design/wiring/screen-cable-study.md:59`). Glass top unchanged (`design/mfg/specs/screen-panel-work/logs/calc.log:7`).
- Board area: the strip, 6.00 mm2 (`design/mfg/specs/screen-panel-work/logs/read_board2.log:31`) instead of 20.44 (`design/mfg/specs/screen-panel-work/logs/read_board2.log:11`); the same re-routing as A.
- Build guide: the joint is made outside the block: panel face down on a soft fixture, the board on its back with the foam, the tail folded round the board end onto the lands, under face up, the hot bar pressing down. The band ribbons cannot use such a posture in place (`design/mfg/assembly/hotbar-joint.md:10`); here the board is still free. The joined panel and board go into the block as one, and the band ribbon joint, made today with the board parts face up on a fixture (`design/mfg/assembly/assembly.md:202`), is then made with the panel under the board: the fixture changes.
- Asks of the maker: a solderable contact end, its pitch, land size and joint process, and whether the maker makes the joint on our board.
- Worse at: no swap without heat, no socket test before joining, a new fixture, the pitch question.

## Judgement (mine; the lead decides)

Option A, in two steps by the panel's thickness. A1 if the maker can hold the panel at 0.5 or less (`design/mfg/specs/screen-panel-work/logs/calc.log:59`): today's routed board stays as it is except J1 and the tracks of its nets, and the only closed board of this design is kept. A2 if the maker cannot: the panel may be 0.80 (`design/mfg/specs/screen-panel-work/logs/calc.log:64`) but J1 and the eight 0201 parts go back to the under face, a placement that ended open in every routing round so far (best 6 unconnected and 3 violations: `design/ecad/ring-route-c/notes.md:72`); that is a new routing round with an unknown end. Why A over B and C in both steps: a standard contact end (the FH35C, already in this design's library, netlist and build guide: `design/ecad/ring/parts.csv:82`), a panel that can be tested and swapped without heat, and no loss of lit length; the glass top stays at 3.1755 inside the 3.20 block (`design/mfg/specs/screen-panel-work/logs/calc.log:7`, `design/wiring/lead-decisions.md:18`); J1 at its 1.0 maximum leaves 0.03 above the floor (`design/mfg/specs/screen-panel-work/logs/calc.log:16`), the room the 1.00 microphones already use (`design/mech/thin/out/v10/before/placement.csv:11`). B is the fall-back if the maker cannot make an FH35C contact end of at most 5.0 width (`design/mfg/specs/screen-panel-work/logs/calc.log:45`); C only if no tail can fold at the hand end. So the first question to the maker is the thinnest panel it can hold.

RESULT: done, three connection options are worked out from the thin model and the routed board, each in a variant that keeps the eight screen-face parts (a thinner panel) and one that moves them under the board (a board that never closed so far), and option A1, where only J1 moves, is judged best if the maker can make the thinner panel, A2 otherwise, for the lead to decide.
