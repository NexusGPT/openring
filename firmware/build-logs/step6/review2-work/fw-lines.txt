FW l.924: ### 8.3 Battery measurement
FW l.925: 
FW l.926: - The nPM1304's system monitor measures the battery voltage, the battery current and the thermistor [NPM p99, section 7.1]. The charger sensor driver gives voltage, current and temperature channels (`SENSOR_CHAN_GAUGE_VOLTAGE`, `SENSOR_CHAN_GAUGE_AVG_CURRENT`, `SENSOR_CHAN_GAUGE_TEMP`) [SRC 11; that the family driver serves the nPM1304 is TO CONFIRM, section 10.5 item 3]. Each sample feeds `nrf_fuel_gauge_process()`, which returns the state of charge [SRC 12]; `nrf_fuel_gauge_tte_get()` and `nrf_fuel_gauge_ttf_get()` give time to empty and to full [SRC 12].
FW l.927: - **The motor's charge is added.** The chip measures the battery current inside its own path [NPM p99]; the motor draws from the cell through U11, outside that path (section 3.1). The motor player sums the charge of every pulse since the last sample (t_on x 80 mA + 10 ms x 175 mA per pulse, section 3.1; the allowance is an **[ESTIMATE]** until bench step B-M3 measures the start). The current fed to the gauge is I_fed = I_measured + Q_motor / t_sample, counted as a discharge [CALC example: one MF, 51.50 mA.s, in a 1 s sample adds 51.5 mA to that sample]. The sign the library expects for a discharge is TO CONFIRM (section 10.5, item 32). U11 is a linear regulator, so its input current equals the motor current within its own small ground current (motor-drive.md section 2.5).
FW l.928: - Sampling: every 1 s in the recording states (thresholds use the mean of the last 10), every 60 s in the other awake states (thresholds use the single sample). Every sample also reads the battery current limit status (section 8.7).
FW l.929: - The battery current during a discharge has a full scale of 140 mA [NPM p99]; the chip path stays at or under 55.7 mA with every load on (architecture 17.22).
FW l.930: - The fuel gauge needs a battery model of the actual cells; Nordic makes it with its nPM PowerUP application [NPM p99]. One model per ring size or one for all is open (section 10.5, item 4).

FW l.932: ### 8.4 Ship mode entry and exit (nPM1304 ship task after VBUS removal, [NPM p116])
FW l.933: 
FW l.934: The nPM1304 enters ship mode through its task register TASKENTERSHIPMODE [NPM p118]. Its specification states: "When VBUS is not present, the device enters Ship mode immediately. The host software must wait until EVENTSVBUSIN0SET on page 136 to ensure VBUS is disconnected and discharged before writing to the register" [NPM p116]. EVENTSVBUSIN0SET holds the event EVENTVBUSREMOVED, "VBUS removed" [NPM p136]. There is no timer: the firmware does not use hibernate (TASKENTERHIBERNATE), and F4's timed hibernate is removed.
FW l.935: 
FW l.936: Entry, in this order, for `SHIP` (FACTORY sub-commands SHIP_ENTER and SHIP_SAMPLE, section 9.3) and for `OFF` (menu "Power off", or B2):
FW l.937: 1. Close everything (recording, storage, USB, radio, screen); save the settings and the time in RRAM. SHIP_ENTER first clears the factory flag; SHIP_SAMPLE keeps it at 1.
FW l.938: 2. Write SHPHLDCONFIG with the default press time (96 ms) and trigger TASKSHPHLDCFGSTROBE: the specification requires both before ship mode [NPM p116, p118].
FW l.939: 3. If VBUS is present, wait for the event EVENTVBUSREMOVED (register EVENTSVBUSIN0SET, [NPM p136]), reported on PMIC_INT. While it waits the ring does nothing else: no screen, no radio, no storage, the USB stack is disabled on `UDC_EVT_VBUS_REMOVED` (section 8.6). Only the factory commands reach this step with VBUS present: every row into `OFF` starts from a state without VBUS (section 1.2).
FW l.940: 4. Write TASKENTERSHIPMODE = 1 [NPM p118]. The chip isolates the battery and the main chip loses its supply.
FW l.941: 5. If the main chip still runs 1 s after step 4 **[CHOICE]**, it logs the event and goes back to step 3 once; the jig of section 9.3 sees advertising stop or not and reports a fail.
